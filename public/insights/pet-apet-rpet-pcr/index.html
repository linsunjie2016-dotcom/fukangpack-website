PET, APET, rPET and PCR: what the terms mean

These terms describe different things. Separating polymer, form and recycled origin makes a material brief much clearer.

Discuss your project ↗
FUKANG Packaging Insights · Updated 30 September 2026

Four terms, four different questions.

PET is the polymer family: polyethylene terephthalate. APET describes an amorphous form of PET; the “A” does not mean recycled content.

rPET means recycled PET. PCR means post-consumer recycled origin and should be paired with the polymer name, such as PCR PET or PCR HDPE. A claim about recycled content needs a defined material route and evidence.

When a buyer asks for “eco PET,” the useful next question is whether they mean recycled content, recyclability, a certain polymer form or a market-specific requirement.

What to ask your packaging supplier

  • Which exact material and bottle model are being proposed?
  • What closure and seal combination has been checked?
  • Which use conditions and target markets are covered by the available documents?
  • Can we review a finished sample and current specification?

Bring these answers into your own product validation process before approving the final pack.
